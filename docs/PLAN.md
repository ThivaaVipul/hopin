# Hopin plan

Living document, updated as work proceeds. Last updated: 2026-11-03.
Status: **plan and architecture reviewed (2026-11-03); M0 next.** The design is in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md). No repo or Firebase project yet.

A bus companion for two riders in different towns. It notices when you are on a bus, tells you when your stop is near, shows which bus to take and where to board, and learns your regular rides over time. Personal use only, on Android, on free services.

## Decisions (2026-11-03, grilling session)

- **Purpose:** a personal app for two riders. Not on the Play Store, not shared with drivers or conductors.
- **Why:** one rider is new to the area, rides buses often and can't read the Sinhala boards, so finding the right bus and knowing where to get off are hard. The other rides daily, around town and on long trips, where there is no live bus data.
- **No camera** anywhere in the app.
- **Ride detection (hybrid):** Android's activity recognition says "in a vehicle"; the app's own logic (stop-start rhythm, speed, where it halts, past rides) decides it is probably a bus and sends a quiet notification ("On a bus?"). One tap confirms or corrects it, and corrections improve later guesses.
- **Features, in this order:**
  1. **M1, get-off guide + ride detection:** works from day one with GPS alone. Every ride is logged from here on.
  2. **M2, which bus to take:** route numbers, where to board, and the board's end town in big letters (Tamil first, with Sinhala and English under it; your change in M2's grilling), so he can match the board without reading Sinhala.
  3. **M3, learned commute:** when buses usually pass, how long rides take, when to leave.
- **A standalone app** in its own repo. If M2 finds a usable route source, a scheduled GitHub Actions job collects it.
- **Data:** synced through Firebase on the free Spark plan (no card). Pooled between the two riders: learned routes, stops and board names. Private to each person: ride history. Live position is shared only when the rider taps "Share this ride".
- **Stop alert:** a vibration. A spoken line (in the app's language) is an optional setting, off by default, and is dropped if it turns out to be hard.
- **Language:** English by default, Tamil as a switch in Settings (your change, 2026-11-03). Sinhala appears only for board and stop names, beside the English or Tamil.
- **Name:** Hopin ("hop in"). Short and playful; no -ly names.
- **Look and feel (2026-11-03):** a smooth UI and UX with purposeful animations and a proper colour system (light and dark), at a senior quality bar. Before M1a's screens, a look pass renders a few directions from real widgets to pick from.

### Defaults (accepted 2026-11-03, change any of them)

1. Flutter, Android only (min SDK 26), arm64 APK, in a **public** GitHub repo `ThivaaVipul/hopin` (public since 2026-11-03: unlimited free Actions minutes and branch protection; nothing personal goes in it, see CLAUDE.md).
2. **Installs: GitHub Releases** (settled in M0c): each release puts the signed APK on the public repo's Releases page, and Hopin offers the update when a newer one is out (from M1a). No cloud service account, and builds never expire. (Firebase Hosting on the Spark plan refuses APK files; App Distribution was the alternative.)
3. Google sign-in; only the two riders get in. Their accounts carry a `rider` claim that you set (M0b's change: no account IDs or emails in the repo, and no rules change to add a rider). Yours is set after your first sign-in, the second rider's later.
8. **Firebase account:** your personal Google account (the Firebase CLI's login on this PC), Spark plan, no billing.
4. Map: **MapLibre** (`maplibre_gl`) drawing **OpenFreeMap** vector tiles, recoloured to the app's look (changed in the look pass, 2026-11-03: free with no key and no limits, smooth, restylable; it replaces `flutter_map` with plain OSM image tiles). Place names in English, Tamil and Sinhala come from OSM where present.
5. Destination for the get-off guide: a saved place (Home, Uni, ...), a search, or a pin dropped on the map.
6. Local first: the phone keeps its own database and syncs through Firestore, so everything works offline on a bus with a weak signal.
7. A professional setup: CLAUDE.md, docs, strict analysis, slim CI on pull requests only, Conventional Commits, automatic releases. Every git step and the Firebase project creation wait for your OK.

### Get-off guide (M1a grilling, 2026-11-03)

- **Sign-in:** one-tap Google sign-in on first launch, then the app works at once. Syncing waits quietly until the rider claim is there, then catches up.
- **Home:** map-first: the map at your position, a "Where to?" bar, saved places as chips under it.
- **Search:** Nominatim, typed then searched (its policy forbids search as you type). Each result shows its English or Tamil name with the Sinhala name beside it (OSM's `name:*` tags). Photon was the alternative: suggestions while typing, but without reliable Tamil or Sinhala names.
- **Alerts:** 1 to 3 stages in Settings, each with a distance (3 km, 2 km, 1.5 km, 1 km, 700 m, 500 m, 300 m, 150 m) and a vibration pattern (about 6, each with a "try it" button). Defaults: "Get ready" at 1.5 km with a gentle double tap, "Get off next" at 400 m with a strong long buzz. On a fast bus a stage fires early enough to give at least about 45 seconds.
- **Ride end:** by itself once within about 100 m and walking or still for a minute; an urgent "You passed your stop" about 300 m past it. End is always one tap, on the ride screen and in the notification.
- **Ride screen:** the map with you and the destination; a sheet with the distance left in big type, the time left, progress, the destination's Sinhala name, "Now near <place>" and End.
- **Defaults:** saved places from any destination (a star and a name: Home, Work, Uni or your own), managed in a list (rename, reorder, delete). Settings: language, alert stages, the spoken line (off), the phone's battery guide, sign out, version and "check for updates". Updates: a calm banner on Home plus one notification per new version. The maker's battery guide shows the first time a guide starts. M1a asks only for location while the app is in use and for notifications; "Allow all the time" waits for M1b. "Now near" refreshes about once a minute from Nominatim, cached; the map caches the tiles it has shown, with no offline download yet.

### Home and places (M1a2 grilling, 2026-11-03)

- **Location:** the first time Home opens, a board card gives the reason in one line, then Android's own popup ("while using the app"). If refused, the map still works (around Colombo) and a chip offers location again.
- **Tapping a place** (saved, searched or a dropped pin) opens a destination sheet: the name in English or Tamil, the Sinhala name in amber, the distance from you, and Save or Edit. Start joins it in M1a3.
- **Editing places:** long-press a chip on Home to rename it, change its icon (home, work, uni, other) or delete it. A full list with reordering comes with Settings in M1a4.
- **Recents:** the last 6 destinations, on this phone only. Saved places sync, private to you.
- **Defaults:** the map follows the phone's dark mode (Board light and Board dark styles, generated from OpenFreeMap's by a script in the repo); long-press the map to drop a pin, named after the nearest place; a full-screen search favouring Sri Lanka and your area, each result with its name, Sinhala name and town, at most one request a second and cached; your position as an amber dot with a board ring, drawn by the map; places are the first synced data (local database first, uploaded when you are a rider and online), with their own rules and tests.

### The guide (M1a3 grilling, 2026-11-03)

- **Ride mode on the same map:** Start, in the destination panel, turns the panel into the ride board (the Sinhala name in amber, distance and time left, the board lights as progress, "Now near", End) and slides the search bar and chips away; End brings them back. One map, never reloaded.
- **It keeps guiding** when the app is swiped away or cleared: the guide runs in its own foreground service, with an ongoing notification (distance left, End).
- **Notifications:** at the first Start, a one-line board card says why, then Android's popup.
- **Locked phone:** the stage's vibration pattern plus an alert notification on the lock screen (no sound, screen stays off).
- **Patterns:** Gentle double tap, Strong long buzz, Heartbeat, Triple knock, Rising, Waves; plus a fixed, strongest pattern for "You passed your stop".
- **Defaults:** "Get ready" at 1.5 km (gentle double tap) and "Get off next" at 400 m (strong long buzz), each once per ride and early enough on a fast bus to give about 45 seconds (editing them comes with Settings, M1a4); with the app open, the board turns amber at "Get off next". GPS every 5 seconds, only during a guide; time left from your recent speed, hidden while still; "Now near" about once a minute. Arrived: within about 100 m and walking or still for a minute ends the guide with a gentle tap. Missed stop: having been within 400 m, 300 m further on fires the urgent pattern and a "You passed your stop" alert, and the guide keeps going so you can head back. The first Start asks Android to exempt Hopin from battery optimisation and shows the maker's steps (Xiaomi: Autostart, "No restrictions"; Samsung: "Never sleeping apps", "Unrestricted"), with a button to the settings where Android allows it. The spoken line waits for Settings (M1a4) or is dropped; ride history comes with M1b. Ships as v0.3.0.

### Settings and updates (M1a4 grilling, 2026-11-03)

- **Settings:** one page, from the person button on the "Where to?" bar (it replaces the account sheet): you (name, rider access), Places, Alerts, Language, Battery guide, Updates (version and Check), Sign out.
- **Language:** English or Tamil, switched in Settings and applied at once to every screen and the guide's notifications. Saved on the phone and in your profile, so a reinstall keeps it; the sign-in page stays in English.
- **Alerts:** 1 to 3 stages, sorted by distance and named by their order: the closest is "Get off next", the others "Get ready". A stage opens its distance chips (3 km, 2 km, 1.5 km, 1 km, 700 m, 500 m, 400 m, 300 m, 150 m; 400 m added because it is the default) and the six patterns, each with Try, which plays it as the alert does (twice). Changes apply from the next Start and follow your account.
- **Spoken line:** left out; looked at again after the field test (M4).
- **Updates:** release builds check GitHub Releases when Hopin opens, at most every 6 hours; a new version shows a calm banner on Home. Tapping it downloads the APK inside Hopin with progress, then opens Android's installer (the first time, Android asks to let Hopin install apps). No update notification.
- **A guide that never arrives:** after 20 minutes without getting closer, a notification asks "Still going to <stop>?" with Keep and End, and no answer keeps guiding; after 2 hours without getting closer, the guide ends itself with a gentle tap. A long trip that keeps getting closer is never asked.
- **Places:** the list (rename, change the icon, delete) reorders by dragging, and Home's chips follow your order; tapping a place opens the same editor as a long press on its chip. The order syncs, so the places rules gain a field, test first.
- **Defaults:** signing out ends a running guide; Settings shows when notifications are off, with a button to turn them on; the battery guide shows whether Android lets Hopin run in the background, then the maker's steps; the version comes from the build. Ships as v0.4.0.

### Ride detection (M1b grilling, 2026-11-04)

- **Turning it on:** after the update, one board card on Home ("Hopin can notice your bus rides"): Turn on walks through Physical activity (Android's popup) and location "Allow all the time" (Android's settings page); Not now hides the card for good. Settings gains a "Notice bus rides" switch that shows the real state and walks the same steps.
- **"On a bus?":** its buttons are your first two saved places and Not a bus. A place button means yes and starts the guide to it from the lock screen; with fewer places, Yes fills the gap. Tapping the notification opens Hopin to pick another stop.
- **Rides page,** from Settings: rides by day with the time, from and to (area names), duration and a Bus, Not a bus or Unsure label; a ride opens its path on the map, its label (changing it teaches the logic) and Delete.
- **Not a bus:** GPS stops at once; the ride keeps its time, label and the logic's numbers, no path.
- **Keeping rides:** until you delete them.
- **Defaults:** no question while a guide you started is running (the ride is logged as a bus); an unanswered ride keeps its path as Unsure, to label later; with the app open during a ride, Home shows the same question as a card; halts are saved with each ride, and pooling them into shared stops waits for M1c; battery is measured over a normal day, and GPS samples less often if detection costs too much. Ships as v0.5.0.

### Rides page (M1b2, 2026-11-04)

- **A ride opens its own page:** its path on a map at the top (halts as small dots, the start and the end marked), then the date, times, duration and distance, the label (Bus, Not a bus, Unsure) and Delete. Back returns to the list.
- **Names:** "from" and "to" are your saved place's name when the ride starts or ends within about 300 m of it, otherwise the area around it (Nominatim's suburb or village, in the app's language). Worked out once when Hopin keeps the ride, and again when the Rides page opens for any ride still unnamed (offline at the time, say). Not a bus rides have no path, so no names.
- **Delete asks first:** "Delete this ride?" with Delete and Cancel; then back to the list.
- **Battery:** a normal day with detection on, starting now on v0.5.0 while the page is built. In the evening you note Hopin's share in Settings > Battery, and with your OK then I read Android's battery stats over adb (GPS time, wake-ups, Hopin's estimated drain).
- **Defaults:** Settings gets a Rides section with one line that opens the list. The list is newest first, under Today, Yesterday and then dates; a row shows the start time, from and to (the distance until named), the duration and the label. Marking a ride Not a bus later follows the rule above: its path and names go, its numbers stay, and it asks first since the path can't come back. A ride's map is its own small map in the Board look. No rules change: the names are already allowed. Ships as v0.6.0.

### Sharing and stops (M1c grilling, 2026-11-04)

- **Who watches:** the other rider, in Hopin, and anyone you send a ride's link to, on a small web page (Firebase Hosting, free on Spark; it serves the project's web settings itself, so no key enters the repo).
- **Starting a share:** Share on the ride board during a guide, or on a slim ride bar on Home during a ride Hopin noticed ("On a bus", the time so far, Share and End), opens Android's share sheet with the ride's link, so a WhatsApp or SMS message is how someone finds out. Tapping the ride's notification opens Hopin there: Android doesn't let a notification button open the share sheet. Rides also share themselves when a guide runs to a place you switched on for it (a switch on each place's edit sheet).
- **The link:** a new, unguessable link for each ride, dead once it ends. An automatic share reaches only the other rider in Hopin, since nobody sends its link.
- **The other rider:** opening Hopin during your share shows a board card on Home ("Shared ride: to Colombo Fort, 12 min") with Watch, which shows you live on their Home map with your stop, until the ride ends or they close it. Without a push service, Hopin can only tell them while it is open.
- **The web page:** a map in the Board look (OpenFreeMap) with the live position, where you are going and the time left (when a guide runs; a noticed ride has neither), "updated 20 s ago", then "Ride ended". English, or Tamil when the browser is in Tamil. No path and no name: the share itself holds no name.
- **Ending:** with the ride or the guide, or Stop sharing on the ride board. The position is wiped at once; nothing of the share is kept.
- **Learned stops:** halts of 10 to 90 s on rides labelled Bus; when halts from two different rides fall within about 30 m, it is a stop for both of you. A busy traffic light may slip in; you can remove it.
- **OpenStreetMap's stops:** Hopin starts from OSM's bus stops around where you ride (the Overpass API, free, fetched once per area and refreshed now and then; OSM already lists 60 to 170 stops in each area checked), with their names. A halt within 30 m of one counts towards it instead of making a new stop.
- **Showing stops:** small dots on the Home map at street level; tapping one opens the destination panel with its names and Start. A learned stop is named once after the nearest place at street level (Nominatim), and either of you can rename or remove it.
- **Defaults:** a share updates about every 10 s while moving and once a minute while halted; the page says "Lost contact" after 10 minutes without an update (the ride service stopped by the phone, say). Anyone can read one share by its link, nobody can list them except the riders, and only its rider writes it. The web page is deployed by hand with your OK, like the rules. Each bus ride's halts go into a pool both riders can read, and each phone works out the stops from the pool, so two phones never fight over a counter and it works offline; deleting a ride or relabelling it away from Bus takes its halts back out. A removed stop stays removed. In two pull requests: M1c1 live sharing (v0.7.0), then M1c2 stops (v0.8.0).
- **Stops, as built (M1c2):** OSM's stops are fetched in squares of about 5 km around your saved places, around where your rides halt, and around any area you zoom into at street level, each square once and again after a month. A bus ride's halts of 10 to 90 s go into a pool both riders read (one record per ride, taken back out when the ride is deleted or no longer a bus), and each phone works out the stops from the pool. Renaming or removing a learned stop is a shared note at its spot, so it still holds when more rides shift the stop a few metres. A learned stop's name is looked up once, at street level, when its panel first opens. Stop dots show from street level (zoom 15) in.

### Which bus (M2 grilling, 2026-11-05)

- **Where routes come from:** OpenStreetMap's bus routes that carry a number; NTC's long-distance timetables (its text sheets: the number, the two end towns, the class and each end's departures; the scanned sheets are left out, and the towns between are read where their headers match a town along the way, your change in M2a3); LMT's Metro routes with their timetables; and the routes you two teach Hopin, from tagged rides and by hand. A weekly GitHub Actions job in this repo (`scripts/data/` and its own workflow; no new repo, project or account) gathers the first three for the whole island, so nothing in the repo points at your routes.
- **Getting them to the phones:** the job publishes the data as files on a fixed `data` release (not a `v*` tag; a prerelease that is never marked latest, so the updater never takes it for a new version). Phones ask for them once a day and right after an app update (an unchanged file costs next to nothing), from github.com, which has no API limit, and keep them for offline use.
- **Which bus, in the destination panel:** pick a place and the panel lists the buses that take you there, one bus only: boarding and getting off within about 500 m of walking (2 km on a trip over 10 km, your change in M2a3). Where OpenStreetMap maps a route only part of the way, the rest of its road is guessed along real roads (OSRM, through the "via" towns in its name), drawn dashed, and places along it count (M2a3). Each bus shows its number, its board, where to board (a stop on the map) and its next times; tap one to see its route and the stop on the map, then Start as now.
- **The board:** a bus shows its route's end town, not your stop. Hopin shows it in three lines: Tamil large in amber, Sinhala under it, smaller, and English small (your change: Tamil first). The names come from OpenStreetMap's town names, and either of you can change any line on a route, shared between you. The same three lines replace the single Sinhala line on the destination panel and the ride board, so every board in Hopin reads alike.
- **NTC's routes:** they have no road, only their two end towns (and from M2a3 the towns between that a sheet names). One shows when you are in or near one of its towns (about 10 km) and the place is near a later one: its number, board, class (Normal, Semi Luxury, Luxury, AC), that end's next departures and its bus stand from OpenStreetMap. Once one of you rides it tagged, its real road is known and towns along the way match too.
- **No bus known:** "No bus known yet" and the place's name large in Tamil, Sinhala and English, to show a conductor or someone at the stop; the ride you take then teaches Hopin the route.
- **Live LMT buses:** live Metro buses show on the Home map whenever it shows LMT's area, and a Metro route in the panel says when its next bus reaches your stop. They come from a community-run open feed (GTFS-Realtime); when it is down, the timetable's times show instead.
- **Teaching a route:** during a ride the ride bar and the ride board ask "Which bus?": the number, then its end town (one of a known route's two, or a search). A ride can also be tagged later on its page in Rides. A number Hopin doesn't know offers Add right there.
- **Routes in Settings:** a Routes list (search by number) and a route's own page: its road on the map, its ends, the board lines, the towns it passes, and Remove. Add takes the number, the two end towns and a few towns or stops along the way from search; Hopin draws the road between them (OSRM's free routing), and a tagged ride later replaces that guess.
- **Not in M2:** changing buses on the way, NTC's scans, fares, and when buses usually come (M3).
- **Defaults:** each bus shows its next three times, with All times behind them; NTC's normal-day sheets are used, not its special-day ones; where to board is the route's stop nearest you (or the nearest point on its road when it lists no stops); while "Which bus?" is open, the numbers of known routes along the ride so far come first; picking a bus in the panel before Start tags the ride with it; tapping the "No bus known" name shows it full screen. Changes to OpenStreetMap's, NTC's or LMT's routes (board lines, Remove) are shared notes, like the stop notes, so the weekly refresh doesn't undo them; Remove hides a route for both of you. A tagged ride's road is shared with the other rider (only the road, no times), like the halts. NTC's end towns are matched to OpenStreetMap's towns by the closest spelling (the sheets spell towns several ways), and its revisions are found by comparing the folders each week. Live buses are polled every 20 s, only while Home is open over LMT's area. The data files keep their sources apart, so OpenStreetMap's stays under its licence (ODbL), and the app credits each source. In three parts: M2a the data and the panel (the job, then the panel in v0.10.0, more buses in v0.11.0), M2b teaching routes (rules first, v0.12.0 to v0.14.0), M2c live LMT buses (v0.15.0).

### Look (look pass, 2026-11-03)

Three directions were rendered from real widgets on M1a's screens (Board, Ticket, Thambili); you picked **A. Board**: the bus's own destination board.

- **Colours (light):** board `#18241F` (sheets, search bar, chips, tiles), amber `#FFB020` (the destination's name, progress, accents), board text `#EEF2EC`, muted `#93A39A`, page `#F2F4F1`, urgent `#E5484D`. **Dark:** page `#0F1714`, board `#1C2A24`, the same amber, urgent `#FF6B6E`. The map is recoloured to match: pale greys by day, deep green-black by night.
- **Type:** Barlow Semi Condensed for numbers and headings, Barlow for text, Anek Tamil for Tamil, Noto Sans Sinhala for Sinhala names; all OFL and bundled.
- **The signature:** the destination's name large in amber on the board, as it appears on the bus (Sinhala at first; from M2 the Tamil name large, with the Sinhala and English names under it, your change); progress as a row of LED dots; at "Get off next" the whole sheet turns amber.
- **Shapes:** small radii (10 for controls, 18 for sheets), flat surfaces, no decorative shadows.
- **Motion:** numbers roll like a board, the dots light one by one, and the alert flips the board to amber in time with the vibration; a static path when the phone asks for reduced motion.

## What we know (research, 2026-11-03)

- **Live bus data in Sri Lanka:** only in pockets. LMT-GO "Metro Bus" (government, 7 Colombo pilot routes, live) with an unofficial open GTFS and live feed; "SP Bus" for SLTB buses in the Southern Province (Jan 2026). NTC has GPS on 1,500+ private buses but uses it for enforcement only, with no public data. Google Maps has only old Western Province timetables; Moovit doesn't cover Sri Lanka. Neither rider's routes are covered, so Hopin learns from their own rides.
- **Route data:** OpenStreetMap has only 83 Sri Lankan bus routes, mostly around Colombo, tagged unevenly. NTC's timetable page links to Google Drive folders (expressway and normal-way timetables) rather than listing routes, and its route map is an outside site (`203.189.65.204/ntc_routes`). So M2 starts with a data check, not a ready scraper. Town names in Sinhala and Tamil are widely present in OSM (`name:si`, `name:ta`), which gives the board text.
- **Android:** the activity recognition API is free and on the phone, and reports IN_VEHICLE, ON_BICYCLE, WALKING, RUNNING and STILL, but can't tell a bus from a car or tuk; that part is Hopin's own logic. Location in the background needs "Allow all the time" and a location foreground service; that's fine for a sideloaded app, since Play's background-location review doesn't apply.
- **Free packages only:** the well-known `flutter_background_geolocation` needs a paid licence for Android release builds, so it's out. Candidates to check against current docs in M1: `geolocator`, `flutter_activity_recognition`, `flutter_foreground_task`.
- **M2 data check (2026-11-05):** NTC's Drive folders hold 97 timetable PDFs (85 distinct): 64 are Excel exports with real text, 33 are scans with no text. The text sheets read cleanly as tables, but their Sinhala headers come out garbled (an old font encoding), so the end towns come from the English file names and the towns between are left out. The sheets have no version field (a revision is a new file beside the old one, with a date in its name), spellings drift, and none are in Tamil. OpenStreetMap's bus routes are sparse outside Colombo and unevenly tagged (about two thirds carry a number, few an end town), and its bus stops rarely have Sinhala or Tamil names, though its towns do. LMT's Metro buses (7 routes: CM01 to CM06 and CM08) are published by a community-run open server, not LMT itself, as GTFS (220 stops, timetables, shapes) and GTFS-Realtime (positions, arrivals, alerts): 53 buses were live on a weekday morning; no licence is stated, and its live feed has been seen empty at times.

## Risks

- **Phone battery savers:** Xiaomi (MIUI or HyperOS) and Samsung (One UI) stop background apps beyond Android's own rules, and the riders use one of each. The app may need settings changed (Xiaomi: Autostart, battery saver "No restrictions"; Samsung: "Never sleeping apps", battery "Unrestricted"), with a one-time guide in the app.
- **Bus versus car or tuk:** the hybrid confirm covers mistakes; accuracy improves with corrections, not from day one.
- **Battery use:** GPS only during vehicle rides, sampled gently; measured in M1b.
- **M2 data:** a third of NTC's sheets are scans and the towns between can't be read, so those are left out, and its file names spell towns several ways (matched to OpenStreetMap's by the closest spelling). LMT's data comes from a community-run server with no stated licence: if it changes or goes away, the Metro routes and live buses drop out and the rest keeps working. OSRM's public server is for light use only (the weekly job's guesses, a request a second, and one request each time a hand route's towns change), and it can't be told to keep off expressways, so a guess on one is dropped. GitHub pauses a public repo's scheduled workflows after 60 days without activity; a manual run starts the job again. Routes learned from rides and added by hand fill the rest.
